## Claims Process

For in-network care, your provider will typically file the claim on your
behalf, and you do not need to submit anything yourself. The provider
submits the claim electronically to the plan, which adjudicates it against
your benefit design, applies any deductible, copay, or coinsurance, and
issues an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) to you within 10 business days of
processing.

For out-of-network care, or in the rare case an in-network provider does
not file on your behalf, you must submit a claim form along with an
itemized bill within 180 days of the date of service. Claim forms are
available on the member portal or by calling member services. Late
submissions beyond the 180-day window may be denied for timely filing.

Once a claim is received, it moves through three stages: intake and
eligibility verification, medical necessity and benefit adjudication, and
payment or denial determination. Most clean claims (those with no missing
information) are processed within 15 business days. Claims requiring
additional medical records or prior authorization confirmation can take
up to 30 business days.

If a claim is denied, the EOB will include a denial reason code and a
plain-language explanation. Common denial reasons include: service not
covered under the plan, missing prior authorization, provider out of
network without emergency justification, duplicate claim already paid,
or insufficient documentation of medical necessity.

## Appeals

Members have the right to appeal a denied claim within 180 days of the
denial notice. A first-level internal appeal is reviewed by a plan medical
director who was not involved in the original decision. Internal appeals
are decided within 30 calendar days for standard appeals and 72 hours for
urgent/expedited appeals involving an immediate health risk.

If the internal appeal is also denied, members may request an external
review by an independent review organization not affiliated with the plan.
The external reviewer's decision is binding on the plan. Instructions for
requesting an external review are included with every internal appeal
denial letter.

## Reimbursement and Payment

Approved claims are paid directly to the provider for in-network services.
For out-of-network claims submitted by the member, reimbursement is sent
directly to the member via check or direct deposit, typically within 10
business days of approval. Members are responsible for any remaining
balance not covered by the plan, subject to balance-billing protections
under applicable law for emergency and certain facility-based services.

Overpayments identified after a claim has been paid may result in a
recoupment request from the plan, typically applied against future claims
rather than requiring a direct refund, unless the overpayment resulted
from fraud or member ineligibility at the time of service.
